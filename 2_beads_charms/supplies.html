My favorite section!! Have you ever seen the videos of people selling scoops? Some people may refer to the bowl as "soup". If you're also familiar with ASMR, you might see creators using visually stimulating setups with sounds overlayed.

Squishies captured that energy perfectly. They offered a soft, squeezable form of nostalgia: think pastel colors, satisfying smushes, and a mix of childhood comfort with intricate designs. Originally, Taba and similar brands set out to give people that familiar joy: something soft, squishy, and visually cute. Over time the range expanded: from food-shaped charms and simple toys to elaborate, art‑style squishy masterpieces.

───୨Tools I Use─

Bead trays Small scissors Jewelry pliers Round-nose & flat-nose pliers Jump rings Split rings Clasps Charms Elastic cord Fishing line Phone charm straps Wire cutters

I use a mix of pony beads, transparent sparkly beads, seed beads, pearls, and mini acrylic charms. Each type has a slightly different shine and texture, and mixing them helps build cute dimensional patterns.

───୨ৎWires, Strings & Findings─

Elastic cord (1mm) Nylon string Beading wire Fishing line (super strong) Crimp beads Lobster clasps Keychain hardware

Elastic works best for stretchy bracelets, while fishing line is stronger for charms and dangling pieces. Jump rings connect beads to charms, and thin wire helps shape small clusters.

───୨ৎCrafting Methods & Tips─

Stringing patterns Creating clusters Using crimp beads Color-blocking Symmetry & balance Adding charms without breaking the pattern

My process usually starts with a color palette. Then I build a pattern that balances shape, texture, and shine. For phone charms, I use fishing line and double-knot every loop so it stays strong.

Mix bead sizes for visual interest Use clear fishing line for durability Keep colors cohesive (warm/cool palettes) Lay patterns out before stringing Always double-knot elastic Seal knots with a tiny dot of glue Use pliers to secure jump rings Avoid overcrowding charms
